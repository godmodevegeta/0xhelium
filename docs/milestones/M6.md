## M6a: controller

### Measured contract (probes at `9f2839e`)

- **Action space and actuator limits.** The policy outputs `a = tanh(·) ∈ [-1, 1]^12`, and
  `ctrl = default_pose + 0.5·a`. `default_pose ± 0.5` lies inside every actuator `ctrlrange`
  (hip ±0.863, thigh −0.686…4.501, calf −2.818…−0.888 rad). So clipping the final action to
  `[-1, 1]` keeps every target within the trained range and the actuator limits.
  `check_action_bounds` enforces this at run start.
- **Joint order and directions.** The joint order is FR, FL, RR, RL × (hip, thigh, calf). Every hip
  axis is +x. With `mj_kinematics`, +0.1 rad at any hip moves its foot +0.027 m in body y (left).
  +0.1 rad at a calf moves the foot −0.016 m in z and −0.014 m in x. +0.1 rad at a thigh moves it
  −0.026 m in x.
- **Leg extension.** "Calf +1, thigh −0.534" lowers the foot while keeping it under the hip. A test
  verifies this against MuJoCo.
- **Sensor inputs.** The reflex reads actor-observation fields by the policy's declared offsets:
  linear velocity 0, gyro 3, gravity 6, command 45. A test checks the indices and that
  `gravity_y = −sin(roll)`.
- **Timing of a failing shove.** After a failing training shove, the lateral-velocity deviation
  leaves the nominal envelope 40–80 ms after onset. The robot falls 0.34–0.66 s after onset (see the
  baseline columns in the episode tables below).

### Residual reflex (`src/robustlab/adapt/reflex.py`)

The reflex reads five signals from the actor observation (no privileged state):

`f = (gravity_x, gravity_y, gyro_x, gyro_y, linvel_y − command_vy)`, and `d = f − centre`.

A calibrated dead-zone gates it:

`A = smoothstep(clip((max_i |d_i|/e_i − 1 − margin) / (0.25 + width), 0, 1))`

Three fixed joint bases, built from joint names, shape the correction:

- `shift`: every hip +1.
- `roll`: left legs extend, right legs shorten.
- `pitch`: front legs extend, rear legs shorten.

The commanded correction is
`target = clip(amplitude·A·Σ tanh(u_k)·B_k, ±0.30)`. The residual then moves toward it with a
rate limit, `r_t = r_{t−1} + clip(target − r_{t−1}, ±0.05)`, and the applied action is
`a = clip(a_base + r_t, −1, 1)`.

Fixed safety limits: 0.30 action units per joint (0.15 rad of joint target) and 0.05 per 20 ms
tick. With all gains zero, or amplitude zero, `act` returns the base actor's own output buffer.
A 5 s shoved episode is then bit-identical to the frozen policy (integration test, `qpos` equal).

| Parameter | Range | Unit | Seed-0 candidate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `shift_from_lateral_velocity` | ±2 | action/(m/s) | −1.111 |
| `shift_from_roll` | ±10 | action/unit gravity_y | 1.616 |
| `shift_from_roll_rate` | ±1 | action/(rad/s) | −0.957 |
| `roll_from_roll` | ±10 | action/unit gravity_y | 5.996 |
| `roll_from_roll_rate` | ±1 | action/(rad/s) | −0.115 |
| `pitch_from_pitch` | ±10 | action/unit gravity_x | 1.658 |
| `pitch_from_pitch_rate` | ±1 | action/(rad/s) | −0.715 |
| `deadzone_margin` | 0–2 | envelope units | 0.081 |
| `ramp_width` | 0–2 (+0.25) | envelope units | 0.059 |
| `amplitude` | 0–0.30 | action units | 0.159 |

The optimizer chose a nearly minimal dead-zone margin and a steep ramp, so the reflex switches on
soon after a signal leaves the calibrated envelope. The rate limit keeps those switches gradual.
I did not analyse which term produces the recovery; the gains are reported as found.

### Calibrated dead-zone (`examples/m6/reflex-calibration-v1.json`, sha `8a3ed872602f`)

The calibration comes from 11 base-policy episodes of 5 s without pushes:

- commands (1,0,0), (0.5,0,0), (0,0,0), (1,0,±0.5), (0.5,±0.3,0) and (1.5,0,0);
- μ 0.45 and μ 1.0;
- a 2.5 kg payload.

The centre of each signal is its median; the half-width is the largest deviation from the median.
So every calibration tick has abnormality ≤ 1, and the reflex is exactly inactive on all of them
for any margin ≥ 0 (Hypothesis property). `scripts/calibrate_reflex.py` regenerates the file, and
an integration test checks that it reproduces.

| Signal | Centre | Half-width |
| --- | --- | --- |
| gravity_x | +0.0163 | 0.0414 |
| gravity_y | +0.0046 | 0.0934 |
| gyro_x (rad/s) | +0.0006 | 2.6877 (start-up transient) |
| gyro_y (rad/s) | −0.0022 | 1.0671 |
| linvel_y − command_vy (m/s) | +0.0014 | 0.3014 |

**Measured, not assumed:**

- **Calibrated conditions.** On the calibrated nominal commands, the seed-0 candidate had zero
  active ticks (training nominal and validation turn/stand episodes).
- **Uncalibrated condition.** In μ 0.3 + 3 kg, the reflex was active on 41% of ticks for seed 0
  and 5–63% across seeds 0–4. Seed 4's candidate fell there.
- **Before the push.** In the μ 0.3 validation shove the reflex first activated at 0.46 s, before
  the push.

The dead-zone therefore does not keep the reflex off outside the calibrated conditions.

### Objective (`src/robustlab/adapt/evaluate.py`, weights in every candidate record)

All metrics are computed up to the first physical fall. Stopping an episode at the fall therefore
changes no metric (integration test).

The cost of an attack episode is a base term plus two penalties:

- base term:
  - numerical failure: 4;
  - fall: `2 − min(Δt_fall/1.5 s, 1)`, so a later fall costs less;
  - survival: `0.5·min(max tilt after push / 45°, 1)`.
- `+ 0.5·clip(1 − progress/progress_ref, 0, 1)`;
- `+ 0.2·mean|r|/0.30`.

The cost of a nominal episode is:

- numerical failure: 4;
- fall: 3;
- otherwise `2·progress deficit + 1·activation fraction + 0.5·mean|r|/0.30`.

`progress_ref` is the frozen policy's no-push progress for the same physics and command.
`J = mean(attack costs) + mean(nominal costs)`. A numerical failure costs more than any fall, and
`aggregate` counts it separately, never as survival (property test).

### Optimizer and budget

- **Algorithm:** pycma CMA-ES over x ∈ [−1, 1]^10, starting at x0 = 0 (zero gains, i.e. the
  frozen baseline), σ0 0.3, population 8. Samples come from the private generator
  `PCG64(SeedSequence([seed, 6]))`.
- **Defaults:** at most 12 generations, 2000 optimization rollouts and 60 s wall clock.
- **Stopping:** the run stops at the first of generation cap, rollout budget, wall clock or
  cancellation, and records which. A partial generation is never told to CMA-ES.
- **Parallelism:** rollouts run on a spawn-started process pool. Results are consumed in task order,
  and 1, 4 and 8 workers gave identical parameters.
- **Rollouts in a default run:** 1776 = 6 no-push references + 30 baseline (18 training + 12
  validation) + 1728 optimization (12 × 8 × 18) + 12 validation-preview.

### What the measurements do and do not show

**Shown:**

- In this simulator, a small bounded residual on the frozen policy prevents falls in all 8 recorded
  training failures and in 8–9 of 9 validation-preview failures across five seeds.
- The tested nominal walking episodes are unchanged where the reflex stays inactive.

**Not shown:**

- **Generalization.** The validation attacks share failure mechanisms with training, timings are
  correlated through the gait phase, and every attack is a low-impulse shove near the failure
  boundary (9–20 N·s).
- **Stronger shoves and other conditions.** At 150 N the seed-0 candidate still falls on 36 of 254
  timings.
- **Safety outside calibration.** The reflex activates in an uncalibrated nominal condition, and one
  of five candidates fell in it.
- **Promotion.** None of this is a regression gate (M7) or any statement about the physical robot.

### Reproducibility

- **Same seed, different worker counts.** The same configuration and seed gave the same parameters
  and `reproducibility_sha256` with 8 workers, 4 workers and at clean `af859b4`
  (`ea7b9dbc0bbd2b7a…`).
- **What the hash covers.** It includes the deterministic configuration, sets, policy and
  calibration hashes, the reflex schema, the parameters, the stop reason and the per-episode
  outcome digests. It excludes wall time and worker count.
- **Wall-clock stops.** A run stopped by the wall clock is labelled machine-dependent; the
  1-worker run has a different hash for that reason.

## M6b: candidate artifacts and application

### Candidate record and CLI

`candidate.json` (schema 1) is written once with an atomic write; an existing record is never
overwritten. It contains:

- **Identity and status:** `candidate_id = cand-<sha12>`; `status: "candidate"`;
  `regression_gate: "pending: M7…"`.
- **Inputs:** the base-policy sha and "weights unmodified"; the reflex schema, named parameters and
  vector; the calibration and its sha; the sets summary with training ids and the locked-set sha.
- **Search settings:** optimizer configuration, seed, library, x0, decode rule and sampler; the
  objective weights; the baseline and candidate J.
- **Run outcome:** stop reason; generations; rollout accounting (reference, baseline, optimization,
  validation); timing; per-generation history.
- **Measurements:** per-episode baseline and candidate metrics, with summaries, paired change
  classes and costs, for training and validation preview separately.
- **Provenance:** git, scene and versions; the reproducibility hash.

`load_candidate` rejects any record whose status is not `candidate`. The CLI is
`robustlab harden` (see Run it).

### API (extends the existing app; same JobManager, busy guard, worker and replay cache)

| Endpoint | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/hardening` | sets summary, selectable training attacks, defaults, limits, the precomputed sample candidate with its grids, runs |
| `POST /api/hardening/runs` | `{seed?, generations 1–30, focus?}` → 202 with id `hd-<UTC>-<seed>`; 409 while any job runs; 422 for bad input |
| `GET /api/hardening/runs/{id}` | stage, live per-generation progress, record when done |
| `POST /api/hardening/runs/{id}/cancel` | request cancellation |
| `GET /api/hardening/runs/{id or preset-v1}/replay?episode=&ghost=baseline\|matched` | replay (below) |

The replay re-simulates the frozen baseline and the candidate on the identical episode: same model
(including μ or payload), reset, command and push. It checks both outcomes and fall times against
the candidate record (`verification.all_match`). It also returns the candidate's per-frame reflex
activation and affected joints. Locked M7 episodes return 404.

Jobs run on the existing experiment worker. The worker hands rollouts to a bounded process pool
(automatic size `min(8, cpus − 2)`); the wall-clock cap comes from `ROBUSTLAB_HARDENING_WALL_S`
(default 60 s).

### Simulation-first workspace

The app is restructured, not rewritten:

- **Layout.** The header holds the mode tabs. Below it, a two-column workspace puts the active
  mode's controls on the left (about 35%) and one persistent `ReplayViewer` on the right (about 65%).
  Long reports (charts, tables, histories, previous runs) render below the workspace through a
  React portal, so every mode keeps a single component and its state.
- **The viewer is created once.** The renderer, scene and camera exist once per model. A new replay
  only swaps the two robot meshes, so the camera offset and the playback speed carry over, and
  switching modes neither remounts the viewer nor refetches meshes. This was verified: one
  `<canvas>`, the same element, after visiting all five modes.
- **Viewer contents.** The viewer shows:
  - an overlay legend that names each robot and its live status ("upright" / "FELL (tilt > 45°) at
    2.50 s");
  - the shove arrow and its timing, a payload box and a floor-friction cue;
  - a timeline with the push window, fall events and (in hardening replays) reflex activation;
  - the replay source (recorded, re-simulated and matching the record, or a sample);
  - a fullscreen button and the note "Recorded MuJoCo poses · no physics in the browser".
- **Sample replay on load.** First load shows the Hidden Phase verified failure, labelled "Sample
  replay · precomputed preset, re-simulated on load".
- **Automatic replay of new results.** New results load into the stage automatically: a scenario
  run, a discovered failure, or a finished hardening run.
- **Policy Hardening mode.** It provides:
  - the status pill "Candidate — pending regression evaluation (M7)";
  - an episode selector (training and validation preview, labelled with the recorded change);
  - a ghost choice: frozen baseline with the same shove, or matched baseline with no shove;
  - **"Replay Baseline vs. Candidate"**;
  - a training-set selector (all 8 confirmed failures, or one), generations, seed,
    **"Harden Controller"** and Cancel;
  - live CPU optimization progress (stage, generation n/N, rollouts, best J against the baseline,
    attacks survived);
  - a baseline-vs-candidate table for training and validation, marked precomputed or executed now.

  Below the workspace it shows the J history chart, per-episode comparison tables (regressions in
  red), exploratory grids, parameters and provenance.
- **Mobile (< 1024 px).** The viewer comes first at 42vh, then the controls. Wide tables scroll
  inside their cards.
- **Visual theme.** The existing interface is light (`#eef1f5` background), not dark. I kept that
  identity and did not restyle it.

## Limitations

- Every attack is a horizontal trunk force on flat ground in one simulator, with one policy and
  deterministic episodes. Seeds vary only the optimizer.
- The training and validation-preview sets are small (8 and 9 confirmed failures), drawn from a
  few failure mechanisms, and correlated through the gait phase. The locked M7 set is listed but
  untested.
- The dead-zone is calibrated on 11 conditions. It activates elsewhere (μ 0.3 + 3 kg), and one
  candidate fell there.
- The candidates are not evaluated on rough terrain, latency, sensor noise or real hardware. The
  reflex mechanism has not been analysed.
- The fast suite now takes 23.7 s against the hook's 30 s timeout.
- The process pool uses 8 of 11 cores. A shared deployment would need a smaller pool or a lower
  generation limit.
